# Surface: Sketchbook gallery (index.html, whole site)

Mode: Experience. Audience: friends and family Priyam shares the link with, plus visitors from his software-engineering portfolio, often on a phone. Job: look through his drawings, open a few up close, come away with "creative engineer". Constraints: only his 13 drawings; Ayush Tanwar's piece removed; no email/phone; own identity, linked to the portfolio only.

## Direction contract

THESIS: The site is his actual sketchbook lying on a desk: a top-bound spiral pad whose pages turn, one drawing per sheet. It refuses the category default of a dark masonry grid with hover overlays and a lightbox.

OWN-WORLD: A deep green self-healing cutting mat is the ground of every screen, with its fine grid and ruler ticks; a black hardboard cover with a school-notebook name label (NAME / SUBJECT / PAGES) bordered in red and blue; silver spiral wire with punched holes; the scans themselves as pages; pencil-yellow (the barrel of a yellow HB pencil) as the one accent; labels set like the stamped lettering on a pencil barrel.

STORY: The visitor opens the cover, flips through 13 sheets, turns the pad sideways for portrait drawings, opens any sheet up close, then reads who drew them (a software engineer in Seattle who builds AI agents) and goes to the portfolio.

FIRST VIEWPORT: The closed sketchbook centred on the mat, as large as the viewport allows, cover label legible; the stamped title "Sketchbook — Priyam Garg" top left; Index, About, Portfolio links top right; a ruler along the bottom edge whose numbered ticks are the page index; a caption line under the book naming the open page; the primary action is the book itself (tap, drag up, arrow key, or "Open").

FORM: Sketchbook (pinned by the user: "how about a sketchbook theme instead where all the sketches are there but they move like the pages of a sketchbook"). seed: none (pinned). A degraded roll did run first (seed:77f3d92f1a27a50d, no catalog reachable) and was overridden by this pin. Signature interaction: pages flip up over the spiral (drag or tap), and the pad physically rotates a quarter-turn for portrait drawings, the way you turn a landscape pad to draw a portrait. Motion grammar: paper weight, ease-out turns about the spiral, no bounces; reduced motion crossfades.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
